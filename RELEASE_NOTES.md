## Selah 0.1.19

Two faults in the audio path — the stretch between your microphone and the transcriber — have been fixed. Both were there from the beginning, and neither could have been spotted from the outside.

### The audio reaching the transcriber is now clean
- **High-frequency sound no longer folds down on top of speech.** Sound arrives from your interface at 48,000 samples a second and the transcriber reads 16,000, so Selah has always had to reduce it. It was doing that without a filtering step the arithmetic requires — and the consequence is not that the high frequencies are lost, which would be fine, but that they are folded back down into the middle of the range where speech lives. Cymbals, sibilance, stage hiss and electrical noise from the desk were all being deposited on top of the voice. Once that has happened nothing can separate them again: as far as everything downstream is concerned, the noise *is* the voice. Selah now filters properly before reducing.
- **The rumble filter no longer amplifies what it was meant to remove.** The filter that strips HVAC hum and stage thump was built with the wrong coefficients — mathematically it was unstable, meaning its own output fed back and grew a little more with every sample. It never became audible as a howl because Selah rebuilt the filter afresh several dozen times a second, which kept cutting the growth off. But it meant the last part of every fragment of audio was being ramped up by as much as thirty-fold before the speech detector ever looked at it, dozens of times a second, all service long. The filter is now the one it was always documented to be: 85 Hz, measured at the textbook response, with its state carried properly instead of being thrown away and restarted.

**What this means for you:** both faults sat upstream of everything else — speech detection, transcription, verse matching, song tracking. Cleaner input can only help them. But we have not yet re-run a full service recording end to end, so this release makes no claim about a specific improvement in transcript accuracy. It fixes two things that were provably wrong. If you have a recording of a service where the transcript struggled, that is now worth trying again.

### Under the hood
- **The tool we use to judge audio changes now actually tests the audio path.** It required a recording already converted to the transcriber's format, which meant it started *after* the conversion and filtering steps — so the two faults above could not have shown up in its numbers however bad they got. It now accepts a recording exactly as it comes off the desk, at any sample rate and channel count, and runs it through the same processing a live service goes through. It can also replay a single channel, matching the "Input channel" setting added in 0.1.18.
